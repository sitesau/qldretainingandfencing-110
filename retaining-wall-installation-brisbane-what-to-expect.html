Where the money moves

The indicative band for timber sleeper work is roughly $300-$450+ per square metre, with final cost tied to material choice and wall face area. That means two low-looking quotes can diverge once access tightens, the fence is fixed above the wall, or a surcharge from driveway or pool loading enters the design. Drainage is not cosmetic in the source guide; subsoil drainage is treated as structural on every retaining quote.

  • Face area drives the early comparison, not a photo of the backyard.
  • Truck access, excavation room and spoil movement decide how cheap materials behave on site.
  • A wall holding a driveway or pool carries extra pressure behind the face.

Approvals, engineering and licensing

The published page says approval and engineering triggers are checked before a price is quoted, but avoids one universal rule because height, boundaries, loading, council expectations and nearby structures can each change the answer. It specifically notes Sunshine Coast council asks for a permit on most retaining walls. It also refers to QBCC-licensed contractors, so the licence class should match the actual bundled scope.

  • Ask what triggered the approval view: boundary proximity, combined height, driveway or pool load.
  • Have an engineer specify posts, footings, drainage or block reinforcement where required.
  • Make the written quote state whether engineering, approvals and inspections are in or out.

A cleaner way to compare quotes

Do not rank quotes until exclusions are visible. Put material, face area, excavation, drainage, backfill, fence interface and approvals side by side. A leaning, bulging or rotted wall may be a repair or replacement brief rather than a clean new build, and bundling retaining with Colorbond, timber, pool barrier or gate work only helps when the junction is designed once.

  • Separate visible work from hidden work in every written scope.
  • Compare like with like after fence height and drainage lines are shown.
  • Treat locality facts as tests: salt air, reactive clay, old fill or river flats change questions, not slogans.
  1. Record the site. Write suburb, rough height and length, access limits, and what sits above or behind the wall.
  2. Split visible from hidden. Ask for materials and fencing apart from excavation, drainage and backfill in the written quote.
  3. Test triggers early. Ask which facts trigger engineering, council approval or inspection before accepting a price.
  4. Compare like with like. Review exclusions, fence scope and drainage together rather than headline material rates alone.
  5. Check licence fit. Where retaining and fencing are bundled, confirm the QBCC licence class matches the work described.
